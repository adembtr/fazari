- Epsilon Indi B, 0.4° vs 0.2°: 3.8× the reference stars per exposure; median cell error × 0.53 (E) / × 0.54 (N); star residuals (68 %) × 0.92 / × 0.94; run time × 1.4.

**Recommendation:** 0.4° cutouts as the new default (rule in `accuracy.py`: the smallest size whose median cell error is ≤ 70% of the 0.2° one for every target, with star residuals at most 15% larger). The default in `pipeline/download.py` is not changed here. What it buys: Barnard's Star: cell error 0.039 → 0.019″ (N), cells calibrated on their own stars 11/15 → 15/15 (the others take the season median), edge losses 23 % → 11 %; Epsilon Indi B: cell error 0.061 → 0.033″ (N), cells calibrated on their own stars 9/23 → 17/23 (the others take the season median), edge losses 17 % → 12 %. What it does not do: move the site season offsets beyond the subset noise (Barnard's Star by ≤ 0.02″ E / 0.03″ N (the 2-exposure 0.2° subset itself: ≤ 0.03 / 0.03″); Epsilon Indi B by ≤ 0.12″ E / 0.06″ N (the 2-exposure 0.2° subset itself: ≤ 0.09 / 0.18″)), so Barnard's +0.13″ N offset is not a calibration-area effect.

## Method

- **Shift**: the headline pair of `meta.json` (two seasons with the same spacecraft orientation, ~1 yr apart, so detector-fixed centroid offsets cancel). Δ = measured − predicted length; σ = `shift_err_arcsec`. ΔPA = measured − predicted position angle.
- **Proper motion**: the joint fit (bright path: all exposures, position + proper motion + detector offsets, parallax fixed to the catalogue) or the weighted fit of the season positions (faint path). σ = the fit error.
- **Season position**: measured − predicted at the mean exposure time of the season. σ = quoted statistical error (scatter of the exposures / √N) ⊕ field-calibration error: the cell offsets are common to all exposures of a season × detector cell, so σ_cal = √Σ (n_c/N)² σ_c² over the cells (fallback cells: 1.25 × median accepted σ / √n_accepted). Reference errors are negligible here (Gaia DR3: < 1 mas; Kirkpatrick et al. 2021: ≤ 13 mas propagated to 2026).
- **Field stars**: `field_stars.csv` from `blink.py` with `FAZARI_OUT` set (identical calibration; the site `meta.json` is reproduced). Percentiles of |residual| per axis: 68 % ≈ 1σ and 95 % ≈ 2σ for a Gaussian.
- **Per-exposure WCS jitter**: rms of the mean star residual of each exposure (≥ 5 stars) about its cell model, minus the star noise in quadrature. It is common to the target and the stars of one exposure.
- **Edge-rejected**: share of the reference stars on a cutout that are < 7 px from its edge (`status = edge`).
- **Wide-cutout experiment**: 2 exposures per season × detector, so the target positions of those runs are noisy (± 0.1–0.2″); the calibration columns compare the same exposures. *Site result: change* applies the run's cell corrections to the site measurement (weights = site exposures per cell); the 0.2° row shows the noise of a 2-exposure subset. A sparse subset can split a season (Epsilon Indi B: 2025-04 and 2025-06). Run times are on a busy machine; compare them only with each other. Detector D4 of Barnard's Star is missing in these runs: with only saturated D4 exposures the detection check drops the detector (`blink.detection_check` builds its detector list from exposures with a forced-photometry fit).
